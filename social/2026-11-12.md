## LinkedIn

The umbrella mistake improved. The wider tests found a tradeoff.

I'm building Hakkillo, a tiny English assistant based on SmolLM2-135M-Instruct, on my CPU.

After a stricter prompt failed, I tried targeted training on ownership and participant roles. Following feedback on X, I reserved whole sentence constructions before training, including “belongs to” clauses, contrasting owners, and sentences with two separately owned objects.

The existing 12 cases stayed as regression checks.

The new experimental checkpoint now rewrites “Lend me your umbrella” as “Could you please lend me your umbrella?” It previously changed “your” to “my.”

The separate results:

• Known ownership errors: 4 → 1 across the original 12 cases.
• Cases using reserved constructions: 21/24 → 22/24 reference matches.
• Broader writing-development set: 43/50 → 42/50 reference matches.

Three familiar ownership failures improved, but the gain on reserved constructions was smaller. Two coordinated-ownership cases still fail.

The broader checks also caught regressions: missed grammar corrections and an email address changed into ordinary words.

These are small synthetic test sets with AI-assisted review, not a general accuracy score. I published the checkpoint and its limitations for inspection; the live browser chat still runs v0.1.

Next, I need to improve ownership without losing the writing behavior that already worked.

What do you do to catch regressions when adapting a small model?
