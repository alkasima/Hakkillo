## LinkedIn

Yesterday, a stranger on X helped improve how I test my AI project.

I'm building Hakkillo, a tiny English assistant based on SmolLM2-135M-Instruct, using my CPU.

I shared a mistake: when asked to rewrite “Lend me your umbrella,” the writing candidate replied “Could you please lend me my umbrella?”

The sentence sounded polite, but it changed ownership.

Akshat asked how I would preserve the owner. Aliii suggested three tests: “Return my umbrella,” “Return your umbrella,” and “Don't lend me your umbrella.”

Today I ran those tests against both published adapters. The writing candidate preserved ownership and the positive or negative request in all three. The original ownership mistake still happens.

The useful change today is in the evaluation: ownership, negation, politeness, and the requested action are now reviewed separately. A fluent answer can still change what someone asked for.

These are four regression examples, not a general benchmark. I haven't retrained the model today, and the browser demo still runs v0.1. The graphic shows the actual candidate outputs from Python CPU testing.

If you use AI to rewrite emails or messages, what detail would you most want it to preserve: who is involved, deadlines, or the strength of the request?
